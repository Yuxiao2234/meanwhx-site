# Rights workflow — practical draft

This is an internal workflow note, not legal advice.

## Before putting a new work on the public site

Ask:

1. Who created it?
2. Has every relevant creator approved publication?
3. Is it already public elsewhere?
4. Does publication reveal manufacturing-ready or commercially sensitive detail?
5. Could public disclosure affect a future patent/design-registration strategy?
6. What reuse, if any, is being explicitly licensed?
7. Is attribution written correctly?

If those questions are unclear, do not publish yet.

## Keep public

Usually appropriate once approved:

- finished essays and poems
- public event descriptions
- selected photographs/renders
- conceptual studies
- low-resolution or non-manufacturing drawings
- public reading lists
- high-level process notes

## Keep private by default

- CAD/source geometry
- fabrication files
- supplier/manufacturer details
- unreleased collections
- private partner correspondence
- contracts
- unpublished invention details
- contributor personal information

## Contributor work

Do not infer a broad license from a contribution.

At minimum, agree in writing on:

- author/creator name
- what may be published
- where it may be published
- whether edits are allowed
- whether the creator can later request correction/removal
- whether any commercial reuse is permitted

Use qualified legal advice before turning this into a formal contributor agreement.
